ReplyMate

A multi-account inbox agent that reads Instagram, WhatsApp and Telegram, drafts replies in its owner's voice, and sends nothing without a human tap.

  • RoleDesign & build
  • ScopeAgent, server, console
  • StatusPrivate / live
  • StackPython · Flask · SQLite

01The problem

Inbox work is triage. Automating it is a trust problem.

A message arrives. The owner reads it, works out what it means, writes a reply, and moves on — dozens of times a day across apps that each want their own attention. Most of that effort is not writing. It is deciding.

Automating the draft is easy. Automating the judgment is not. A wrong message in the owner's voice costs more than a slow one, so the system had to be fast at drafting and completely unwilling to send on its own.

02What it does

Four surfaces in, one queue out. Every reply waits for a person.

A

One queue, four surfaces

Instagram, WhatsApp, Telegram and an owner console feed a single review list. Each message is normalised into one conversation model, per account.

B

Drafts in the owner's voice

A persona profile built from approved examples writes a candidate reply for each thread, matching tone and language rather than sounding like a bot.

C

Stops at the tap

Every draft lands in an approval view. Approve, edit or reject. Nothing leaves the system without one of those three actions.

03The approval gate

The gate is the product. Everything else supports it.

A draft is not a sent message. The queue keeps those two states separate, preserves the original text, and records exactly what the human changed. Rejections matter as much as approvals — they are the clearest signal that the persona drifted.

  1. Inbound

    A signed webhook delivers the message; duplicates are dropped per account.

  2. Draft

    The connector and persona model write a candidate reply, never more than one.

  3. Human review

    Owner approves, edits or rejects. This is the only path to a send.

  4. Send

    An approved text goes back through the platform API, with the decision logged.

Zero unapproved sends. Every outbound message is traceable to a human decision. The log keeps the draft, the edit and the final text side by side, so the agent's behaviour can be audited after the fact.

04How it works

Small stack. Deliberate boundaries.

01

One server, many accounts

A Flask service runs connector workers per account with signed webhooks, per-account secrets and owner-only console routes.

02

SQLite as the ledger

Conversations, drafts, approvals and edits live in one file with per-account isolation and a simple backup story.

03

A weekly persona loop

Approvals, edits and rejections become feedback. A scheduled job refreshes the persona profile every seven days.

05Stack & status

Boring on purpose.

A Python service, one database file, platform connectors and a private console. Less surface area means fewer places for an approval step to be skipped — and a system that stays understandable a year later.
